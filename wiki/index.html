What is Malaria?

Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by parasites transmitted through mosquito bites. It is preventable and curable, but only when diagnosed correctly and at the right time.

Malaria can cause extreme symptoms, including:

  • Fever
  • Chills
  • Fatigue and Weakness
  • Nausea
  • Sweats
  • Muscle Aches

In 2024, there were roughly 282 million cases of Malaria reported globally. Of this number, approximately 610,000 deaths were inflicted. There was an increase of 9 MILLION cases from 2023.

The main reason for the deaths and complications that arose was from a lack in an effective diagnosis method. Current tests detect the HRP-2 protein, however, this protein has gone through consistent gene deletions, making 15% of tests cases falsely diagnosed. Something needs to be done.

What is P. falciparum?

Plasmodium falciparum is a protozoan parasite. It is one of the deadliest strains of malaria, while also one of the most common. It is transmitted by female mosquitoes and when in the human body, it replicates within red blood cells and in the liver, leading to the symptoms associated with malaria.

What is PfLDH?

PfLDH(Plasmodium falciparum Lactate Dehydrogenase) is a metabolic, tetramer enzyme critical to the parasite's energy production and its survival. The protein has an NADH active site in each of its monomers, allowing for increased efficiency in energy metabolism. By detecting PfLDH, our team can develop a highly specific, and effective diagnostic to malaria, because the protein is essential to parasitic survival, and will not be subject to gene deletions like HRP-2.
